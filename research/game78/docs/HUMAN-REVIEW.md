# Editorial review and remaining limits

Reviewed the first 12 sequential outputs in every domain from the SHA provider (96 outputs), additional sequential/shortest/longest examples while developing both providers, and the genuine Godot diagnostic pages. The committed corpus retains the first 50 of all 16 provider/domain combinations, 15 seeded cross-section examples each, extremes and detected repetition. Selection is reproducible, not hand-picked to conceal bad batches.

Corrections from reading actual samples: plural legacy subject/verb agreement and a/an errors; ambiguous occupation label “sewer”; pronoun/habit agreement; unconstrained record-keeper mentors; brittle group-name grammar; contradictory trait combinations; repeated item transfers; incompatible flexible-material marks/repairs; separate site occupancy and structural damage so cleanup does not undo abandonment or repair unrelated components. The origin compact renderer preserves stored memory/tradition and the actual Godot `record_id` projection contract.

Quality conclusions:

* Characters differ in family, work, training, setbacks, motives, traits and possessions. Their tone is restrained. Training and broad failures/successes are not always occupation-specific enough; twelve rows per component remain an editorial limitation rather than a complete narrative engine.
* Ordinary items are genuinely different types/materials/ownership stories, not renamed axes. Some repair/mark combinations are broad craft language rather than construction-specific descriptions. They need further subtype-aware editorial filtering before a commercial corpus.
* Special items are treasured craft objects/heirlooms with multiple owners, not automatic artefacts. No magical property is invented. More unusual special-item themes are a future content-pack choice, not hidden mechanics.
* Sites have linked fictional chronology, source marker identity and coherent damage/occupancy transitions. The repeated `later` construction is readable but formulaic. Present source culture is not evidence of historical creators; historical claims remain explicitly generated fiction.
* Origins vary by memory and practice, including context-qualified walls/port/vegetation/settlement relationships. Exact prose repeats in roughly 6% of 1,000 same-town samples. The pack favours work, repair, household records and communal customs; additional religious/cultural conflict, migration and loss themes need authoring review before claiming years of variety.
* Contract and group domains are useful structured research proposals but much narrower than core histories. Their repeated ownership/account complications and group naming suffix remain visible. They are not active game systems.
* Personal names use source-culture-bound original phonotactic profiles. Three profiles do not establish full Azgaar-cultural authenticity. Do not replace canonical place/culture names or represent these approximations as approved cultural naming.
* Site rumours deliberately remain two fixed, truth-labelled forms; richer fact-conditioned rumour banks are an explicit next editorial priority. Public projection never exposes the verdict or hidden history.

The recommendation is the combined engine/constraint architecture, **not approval of every research sentence as shipping game text**. Native providers demonstrably offered useful engines and schemas, but also geography blindness, language mismatch, replay failure, mechanical system assumptions and prose defects. The adopted hybrid improves breadth while preserving explicit quality limits.
