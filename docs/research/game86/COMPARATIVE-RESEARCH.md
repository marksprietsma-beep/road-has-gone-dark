## Comparative design matrix

“Reference-level” below means established general gameplay knowledge used as inspiration, with fresh web verification blocked. Exact patch-specific numbers are intentionally omitted. Mechanics directly supported above are marked **inspected**.

| Game / model | Useful solution | What TRHGD should borrow | What should not transfer unchanged |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Final Fantasy Tactics** (classic; reference-level) | Individual CT activations, move/action positioning, visible turn/cast timing, job abilities and height | Small encounters with strong role contrast; an expensive spell announces a future spatial problem; distinctive conditional abilities instead of stat inflation | Speed increasing both movement and activation frequency; opaque charge-time surprises; facing/height/jump/build interactions all in V1; job-system copy |
| **Tactics Ogre / Reborn** (timing reference-level; damage source inspected) | Recovery/time-based ordering makes equipment/actions affect when a unit returns; terrain and approach matter | Show the next activation consequence before committing an expensive move; distinguish reach and area control | Copying all recovery penalties or complex damage-factor stacks. Its larger formations cannot validate our three-person stun balance |
| **XCOM2** (specific AP/reaction source inspected) | Movement and firing commitments, cover, reserve fire; exposed space is dangerous | A preview must say what spending a move leaves available; reaction reservation is a budget, not free bonus DPS; directional cover earns movement | Treating every shot as a freely repeatable one-AP action; reproducing lethal squad alpha strikes or pod activation traps in a three-person fantasy party |
| **Pathfinder Kingmaker/Wrath turn-based** (reference-level) | Deep d20 builds, reach, attacks of opportunity, spells and saves; move versus full-attack tradeoff | Typed attacks/defences/resources, explicit effect timing, conditional build hooks, clear combat log | Iterative full attacks punishing every reposition; stacks of small modifiers; feat taxes; out-of-combat buff routines; importing every PF action type into onboarding |
| **Solasta** (reaction schema inspected; broader play reference-level) | Grid-first d20 combat, readable reaction decisions, light/vertical positioning | Reaction prompts with an exact shared budget; spells expose target and LOS requirements | Full vertical navigation/flying and the entire 5e action suite before the horizontal encounter works |
| **Divinity: Original Sin 2** (reference-level) | Flexible AP, surfaces, spatial combinations, armour gating control | Build interactions around visible states; movement has an opportunity cost; a puddle/fire zone is data rather than bespoke scene logic | Two separate armour barriers and control lockouts as V1 defaults; AP stacking enabling one-turn erasure; broad surface simulation |
| **Battle Brothers** (reference-level) | AP, fatigue, contact control, equipment/armour tradeoffs and endurance across encounters | Finite tactical exertion; getting out of engagement is a decision; modest ordinary equipment can matter | Fatigue bookkeeping on every small action, injury/economy depth, lethal campaign churn and large-company assumptions in a three-adventurer first slice |
| **Into the Breach** (intent reference-level; utilities inspected) | Visible intended attacks, displacement and protecting objectives make non-damage turns decisive | A delayed blast advertises its tile and resolution; redirecting an attack or saving an ally is a real result | Perfect deterministic puzzle knowledge for all enemies; tiny puzzle maps as the only model for hidden information and d20 builds |
| **Baldur's Gate 3** (listed mechanics inspected through mirror) | Movement separate from action, shared reaction choices, concentration vulnerability, bonuses and vertical play | Readable turn budget; protect a concentrating ally; Disengage sacrifices offence; separate UI prompt from actual reaction cost | d4 initiative's heavy modifier dominance; bonus-action economy proliferation; explosive item/surface combos; tabletop-distance scale copied into tiles |
| **Battle for Wesnoth** (official manual inspected) | Zones of control, skirmisher exception, terrain defence and retaliation | A front line controls routes without MMO aggro; deliberate exceptions can define a mobile class; disclose hit chance | Its hex adjacency-based ranged combat, multiplayer army attrition or terrain-defence percentages as direct d20 tuning |

## The combined lesson

No one reference solves all of TRHGD's requirements. FFT provides the spatial/cast-time ambition; streamlined d20 provides the build vocabulary; XCOM and Solasta demonstrate explicit action/reaction costs; Wesnoth makes route control tangible; Into the Breach explains why a known future hazard can be interesting before it deals damage. BG3 concentration shows the usefulness of protecting a caster rather than allowing every spell to resolve immediately and safely.

The important disagreement is whether mobility should cost damage. PF-style full attacks and repeatable AP attacks reward standing still. A move-plus-main model lets every ordinary turn include positioning without giving a stationary archer two full-strength shots. Powerful actions can then create their own carefully bounded commitment. This is why the experiment compares whole economies rather than selecting whichever individual rule sounds attractive.

The source-access limitation remains unresolved. Before adopting exact FFT/Tactics Ogre timing formulas, PF licensing text or a retail-game numerical rule, verify the linked primary references in an unrestricted browser. None of the recommendation depends on importing those formulas or their content.
