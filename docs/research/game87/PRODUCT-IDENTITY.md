# Product identity: deliberate adventurers with consequential lives

**Recommendation:** make Character Life a major pillar, delivered through a small causal loop. TRHGD is a party RPG in which deliberate character builds solve tactical expeditions, and those expeditions change whom the adventurers know, what opportunities they can pursue and how their generated home region responds.

The repeatable player decision is: **who goes, what can they contribute, what are we willing to risk, and what will this choice let us do next?** It requires the town, expedition, rules and character history to remember the same people. A different name or a paragraph of biography does not satisfy it.

## The player-facing loop

1. In a hometown, choose a lead for a world problem, personal hook or access opportunity. Show why the lead matters and name the relevant adventurer.
2. Assemble three people with useful builds and relevant history. A relationship can supply a helper; a background can supply knowledge. Neither is required for every encounter.
3. Investigate or prepare. Show what was learned, who contributed and the usable payoff: a known hazard, another approach, a contact, or an encounter preparation option.
4. Resolve tactics through GAME-33's shared engine. A build expresses deliberate training; a life-derived capability has an ordinary machine-readable cost and rule.
5. Return with a durable outcome. At most a small number of salient facts become aspects/relationship/hook changes. Later events recall them, and the player chooses the next build or life response.

An authored skeleton combined with stable generated people/sites gives replay variation without promising an autonomous novelist or a fully simulated society.

## What the alternatives already do well—and the difference to earn

| Comparator | Its useful strength | Concrete TRHGD reason to play | Cost / test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wildermyth | Cast-specific stories, visible change, long lives | Keep ordered multiclass/feat/prestige plans while acquired history changes options in a persistent generated hometown/site network | Must protect builds from unannounced random replacement; prove a callback matters without adding a stat bonus |
| Pathfinder / Wrath | Long deliberate builds and authored campaign encounters | A build's trainer, affiliation or prestige opportunity can be earned from this playthrough's people/sites, rather than always arriving at the same authored chapter | Cannot match its catalogue or authored plot volume; missing a rare opportunity must not brick normal progression |
| Rogue Trader | Dense party capabilities, companions, social power and tactical exceptions | Player-created people accumulate local obligations and histories; identity is not limited to a fixed cast or prescribed campaign position | No promise to reproduce its dialogue volume, setting, ship management or Momentum stack |
| Battle Brothers | Roster decisions, contracts, equipment and costly survival | Preserve individual long-term build investment, give non-damage investigation/illusion roles, and offer recovery/adaptation rather than making replacement the default answer to loss | Three-person parties tolerate attrition poorly; treatment and a substitute must exist before disabling a hero for weeks |
| Generic procedural RPG | Variable map/loot/opponents | Stable causal links: this mentor taught this character after this site event, and this town remembers the resulting obligation | Ten expeditions must produce distinguishable lives without requiring the player to read procedurally padded prose |

These are intended differences, not claims that the current game already delivers them or that other games cannot do them.

## Identity layers and creation

| Layer | Authority | Player meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| People/ancestry, inherited heritage | Identity references + versioned inherited rules definition | Inherited physical assumptions; later mutation never rewrites ancestry |
| Culture, origin, background | World-backed identity and established facts | Shared culture can cross ancestry; background records earlier life |
| Ordered classes, feats, skills, spells, equipment | GAME-32 build and rules pin | Deliberate training, freely combined when legal |
| Occupation, standing, duties | Character Life / campaign entities | Current place in society; can change without taking a class level |
| Relationships, hooks, acquired states | Character Life | What happened and how the player responded |
| Combat snapshot | GAME-32 derivation | Mechanics for a particular revision, not biography or omniscient public knowledge |

**Culture != ancestry; build != biography; class != occupation; background != class; trauma != corruption; mutation != morality.** Reputation has a named subject and audience. Objective truth and what a player/observer knows are separate. Prose renders structured facts. One stable character ID survives change, retirement and death records.

Future creation starts from GAME-81 facts, not a second identity generator. Offer one recommended package per narrative role: Vanguard suggests Roadwarden, Scout/Expert suggest different Wayfinder priorities, Adept suggests Lantern Adept. Label it “recommended first training,” show its actual class, and permit an advanced choice. A Scout may legally become an adept later; the old role remains biography/onboarding metadata.

Minimum creation: confirm identity/ancestry and world-backed origin; choose or accept a background and motivation; accept or adjust one legal first-level build package. Generate **two compatible unresolved hooks** from real references, allow replacing either, and permit an optional third. Offer one plausible starting relationship across the party without forcing romance or a bonus. Short/long ambitions may be suggested from motivation and left undecided. A latent heritage choice appears only if a supported option changes initial mechanics; no giant bloodline catalogue.

Injuries, corruption, pacts, prestige, complicated careers and future transformations enter through play. Personality supplies two optional voice tendencies and contextual reactions; it never removes a sensible response because a generated number says the hero is “always greedy.” Contradictory choices can develop a person. No automatic ancestry-based personality or social morality.

## Failure conditions

This direction fails if the optimal route is farming relationships/mutations, the player must check six maintenance meters before every trip, an event unexpectedly destroys a planned build, or the only difference between two lives is text. It also fails if class choice becomes irrelevant because every event supplies a superior ability package.

The first proof must test both identity axes: identical builds diverge through consequential histories; similar histories still support clearly different deliberate builds.
