# Prototype plan — prove causal lives before a large content library

This is a **designed proof corpus and experiment plan**, not runnable production content or claimed playtest results. It contains24 original template specifications. Their actor/site IDs must bind to fixture or actual generated records; names below are illustrative. A later test harness may exercise all24 using proposed schema fixtures. Production V0 initially enables the16 core templates; the8 advanced templates stay disabled until their stated owners exist. This distinction is deliberate: a research corpus must probe future boundaries without silently implementing corruption, injury or downtime in V0.

## Scope and delivery gates

First build a tiny versioned life store, safe event selector, pending-offer/receipt transaction, projections, callbacks and expedition integration. Use two hooks, coarse relationship edges and nonmechanical aspects. Prove one investigation preparation contract and a two-step personal search through fixtures. Do not build a generic quest planner, full body simulator or broad event library.

Every template package needs: stable definition/version/family; allowed trigger; required roles and finite domains; eligibility AST; two or three choices including a viable decline; exact typed outcome deltas; visibility; memory policy; callback; authored fallback prose; positive and negative fixtures. The table specifies those decisions; implementation must translate them to the contract rather than treating prose as executable content. Common memory defaults are in [EVENT-ENGINE-CONTRACT](EVENT-ENGINE-CONTRACT.md).

## Fixture matrix and negative cases

Prepare three world contexts with stable refs: a mining frontier with a mine/workshop; a river town with shrine/merchant contacts; a ruined-road region with missing facilities. These are fixture definitions for later implementation, not claims about generated worlds already tested. Include available/unavailable/dead mentors, missing helper, conflicting cultures, different ancestries sharing one culture, unknown curse source, slot conflict and two grant sources for the same capability.

For each template, exercise every branch plus: wrong location, stale revision, invalid/unknown pack, hidden knowledge, duplicate commit, cooldown, absent optional/required role and preserved old identity. Reorder candidates and save/reload around each commit boundary. Track event family, actor spotlight, no-candidate reason and resource/standing changes. Archive seeds, inputs, pinned definitions, selected offers, outcomes, hashes and timings by completed group before proceeding. No sampling claim until the harness exists; future runs must preserve failures/no-event outcomes and original case counts.

## Character uniqueness A: ten-expedition counterfactual trace

Two heroes, Mara and Orrin, start with **identical Wayfinder builds**, equipment and progression choices. Their background/hook facts and choices may differ; helper Keva is an existing third hero. This is a designed trace to execute later, not a completed playtest. Same damage totals are acceptable; different consequential options are the test.

| Expedition | Mara's cumulative life change | Orrin's cumulative life change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | E01 records her masonry knowledge at a ruin | E03 records his tracked approach to the same site's outskirts |
| 2 | E04 establishes trust with Keva after a difficult return | E06 records a disagreement about route evidence |
| 3 | E07 binds missing-mentor evidence to a real mine | E10 replaces an old debt with a service duty |
| 4 | E08 opens search with Keva as helper | E12 records fulfilling a separate crossing promise |
| 5 | E09 finds/helps the mentor according to seeded truth | E14 records the town hearing about his crossing help |
| 6 | E02 recognizes workshop marks and opens a contact | E11 elects a family-linked ambition |
| 7 | E13 earns training access via the workshop | E05 explicitly releases a rescue-related debt |
| 8 | E16 recalls the mine search and offers a related lead | E16 recalls the crossing and offers a town contact lead |
| 9 | A different site uses her existing E01 evidence where valid | His E03 preparation enables a different valid approach; no duplicate permanent reward |
| 10 | Mentor/workshop/helper references now change her available opportunities | Family/town/oath references now change his available opportunities |

Both keep the same class/feat totals and combat statistics unless they later deliberately spend an earned training opportunity. A recounting should distinguish them by at least three causal facts and a current decision, without referring to build labels. If changing names makes these lives interchangeable, the design has failed.

## Character uniqueness B: similar history, different builds

Give two fixture characters the same rescued-mentor, trusted-helper and site-discovery history. Build one Roadwarden with defensive training and another Lantern Adept/Veil specialist through legal progression. They can both accept the same contact lead; in the resulting confrontation, one protects an ally through armour/Guard and the other spends Focus on delayed/perception control. Same life evidence does not provide identical spellcasting, free prestige entry without mechanical prerequisites, or the martial's armour expertise. Test legal snapshots/options and observed decisions, not equal duel wins.

A future life-gated prestige fixture then proves conjunction: historical life evidence alone is insufficient without class/skill/feat requirements. Removing today's mentor/graft does not invalidate yesterday's accepted entry, while separately declared current-use restrictions still apply.

## Measurement and stop criteria

**Engineering gates:** all identity/migration/transaction/replay/knowledge invariants pass; no unsupported owner is replaced by narrative claims. Benchmark selection with3 and12 roster candidates on documented hardware; report p50/p95 and worst-case no-match. Initial target p95<50ms for24 templates on the chosen baseline machine is a hypothesis, not measured performance. Profile before caching or broadening content.

**Later formative player study:** recruit6–8 willing testers for two ten-expedition variants (life callbacks enabled versus reduced callbacks), counterbalance order, preserve decisions and record qualitative interviews. This sample guides iteration; it does not establish statistical superiority. Initial review gates: at least5/6 (or6/8) can explain one investigation's contributor/payoff, recall three causal facts for two heroes, and distinguish the two deliberate builds. Record confusion, reload desire, event-reading time, repeated-story fatigue and feeling forced into an optimal romance/mutation. Do not hide negative sessions or average away a build-destroying event.

**Mortal consequence experiment later:** compare explicit rescue/retreat injury, rare survival charge and permadeath policies using matched encounters/known consequence previews. Measure continued play, desire to reload, attachment and perceived agency, not merely deaths prevented. No final death policy follows from this research alone.

If callbacks remain cosmetic, improve a smaller core's consequences before adding classes or events. If content rarely finds an eligible cast, loosen unnecessary helper/site constraints and expose blocked leads. If maintenance overwhelms expeditions, cut duties/meters. If life grants dominate deliberate builds, reduce their scope/cost efficiency before expanding corruption or transformations.
