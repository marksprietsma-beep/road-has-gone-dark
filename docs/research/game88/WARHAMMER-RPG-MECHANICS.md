# GAME-88 — Warhammer tabletop RPG mechanics relevant to TRHGD

**Status:** design research, not production implementation  
**Date:** 2026-10-08  
**Purpose:** identify Warhammer tabletop RPG mechanics that can strengthen The Road Has Gone Dark without importing Warhammer IP, prose, setting identity, or exact copyrighted tables.

This document complements DND35-CHARACTER-OPTIONS-CATALOGUE.md. D&D 3.x is especially useful for **deliberate build breadth**; Warhammer RPGs are unusually useful for **lived consequences, careers, injury, corruption, downtime, social position and campaign pressure**.

---

# 1. Systems surveyed

Primary emphasis:

- Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay (especially 4th Edition, with historical reference to earlier editions);
- Dark Heresy;
- Rogue Trader;
- Black Crusade;
- Imperium Maledictum;
- selected modern Warhammer RPG material where it clarifies corruption, augmetics, investigation or character consequence.

Supporting official/first-party sources include Cubicle 7 product pages, rulebook previews and designer articles, plus archived Fantasy Flight Games designer material.

This is **mechanical inspiration only**. Nothing in this file grants permission to reproduce proprietary Warhammer rules text, setting names, tables, mutations, careers, talents or lore.

---

# 2. Executive conclusion

Warhammer RPGs reinforce the emerging TRHGD design direction very strongly.

The most valuable ideas are not specific careers or mutation tables. They are the structural loops:

    WHO YOU WERE / WHAT YOU DO
            ↓
    career, occupation, social place, patron, duties

    WHAT HAPPENS TO YOU
            ↓
    fear, injury, disease, corruption, mutation, debt, faction consequence

    WHAT YOU CHOOSE TO DO ABOUT IT
            ↓
    recover, hide, adapt, exploit, change career, seek help, make a bargain

    WHAT YOU BECOME
            ↓
    new capabilities, scars, obligations, status, relationships, reputation

This is an excellent complement to GAME-32.

D&D-style class/feat/prestige progression can remain the player's deliberate mechanical build.

Warhammer-style **career, consequence, status and downtime** can help make that character feel like someone living in a dangerous world rather than a build sheet moving between combats.

# 3. WFRP careers: progression tied to fiction

Warhammer Fantasy Roleplay's career model is one of the strongest references for TRHGD outside D&D.

A career is not merely a combat class. It represents a character's place in the world, the skills and opportunities currently available to them, and often their social standing.

Cubicle 7's WFRP material explicitly emphasizes that Talents available through a career are connected to what the character is actually doing in the fiction. Learning outside those normal opportunities can require unusual learning or downtime access.

## TRHGD lesson

Do **not** replace GAME-32 classes with WFRP careers.

Instead preserve three separate layers:

    BACKGROUND
    what you were before adventuring

    MECHANICAL BUILD
    class / multiclass / prestige / feats / skills

    CURRENT LIFE ROLE
    guild job / occupation / faction office / social position / apprenticeship

Examples:

    Background: mason's apprentice
    Build: Roadwarden 4 / Wayfinder 2
    Current role: guild caravan guard

or:

    Background: temple scribe
    Build: Lantern Adept 5
    Current role: assistant to the town apothecary

A current life role can affect:

- training access;
- contacts;
- facilities;
- income;
- downtime actions;
- reputation;
- event eligibility;
- unusual feat/prestige access.

This lets the world matter to character growth without hard-coding every occupation into a combat class.

---

# 4. Career changes should mean something happened

WFRP explicitly encourages thinking about the fictional reason when a character changes or re-enters a career.

That is an extremely strong fit for TRHGD.

A mechanical change should sometimes have a world explanation:

    Want advanced healing training?
    Find a healer, temple or mentor.

    Want a rare weapon style?
    Join, impress or betray an organisation that teaches it.

    Want a corruption-linked prestige path?
    First survive the relevant exposure.

    Want an unusual talent?
    Spend downtime studying with a specialist.

This does **not** mean every level-up requires a fetch quest.

The useful principle is:

> Rare or identity-defining options can be made meaningful by tying eligibility to the world rather than presenting the entire rules catalogue in a menu from level 1.

This supports GAME-32's prerequisite AST and GAME-85's future facilities.

# 5. Ambitions and motivations

WFRP 4e gives characters short- and long-term ambitions, and party-level ambitions are also part of character/campaign structure.

This is directly relevant to GAME-87's proposed hooks.

## TRHGD adaptation

Each adventurer could eventually have:

    motivation
    short_term_goal
    long_term_goal
    unresolved_hooks[]

Examples:

- prove I can survive outside my family trade;
- recover my mentor's missing journal;
- become recognized as a master healer;
- rid myself of a curse;
- earn enough influence to protect my hometown;
- uncover what happened at a particular ruin.

Goals can:

- weight event selection;
- generate expedition leads;
- unlock personal quests;
- alter relationship events;
- provide small non-XP rewards when completed;
- evolve rather than remaining static flavor text.

Do not turn them into mandatory quest checklists.

# 6. Between-adventure Endeavours

WFRP's Between Adventures / Endeavour structure is one of the most useful campaign-loop references for TRHGD.

The key idea is **scarce downtime attention**.

A character cannot do everything between expeditions.

Possible TRHGD downtime activities:

- recover from injury;
- seek treatment;
- train a feat/skill;
- investigate a hook;
- research a site;
- work for income;
- maintain a social/faction role;
- craft or repair equipment;
- cultivate a relationship;
- cleanse corruption;
- adapt to a mutation;
- seek a graft/prosthetic;
- recruit or train a guild member.

This creates opportunity cost without needing enormous resource micromanagement.

## Recommended principle

Use **time and opportunity** as costs, not just gold.

A rare cure is more interesting when it costs:

- three weeks of recovery;
- access to a specialist;
- a favor;
- missing another opportunity;

than when it costs exactly 500 coins.

---

# 7. Social status as world state, not just wealth

WFRP separates social standing from simple cash and uses status to create obligations as characters rise.

TRHGD can benefit from a lighter version.

Possible scopes:

- local standing;
- professional standing;
- faction rank;
- guild office;
- noble/legal privilege;
- religious standing.

A character can be:

- wealthy but socially disreputable;
- poor but respected;
- famous in one region and unknown elsewhere;
- trusted by one faction and hated by another.

This reinforces GAME-87's recommendation to avoid a single universal reputation bar.

---

# 8. Duties and responsibilities

A particularly good WFRP idea is that higher social/career position can create **obligations**, not only bonuses.

TRHGD equivalents might include:

- guild officers owe time to administration;
- temple initiates owe service;
- nobles owe levies or political attendance;
- faction agents receive assignments;
- prestige orders impose codes or duties;
- patrons expect favors.

This solves an RPG problem where every advancement only increases freedom/power.

Higher standing can instead mean:

    more access
    +
    more responsibility

That is fertile event-generation material.

---

# 9. Critical injuries and body consequences

Warhammer RPGs have a long tradition of critical injuries, body locations and lasting physical consequence. Modern WFRP material continues to provide critical-injury options, and Imperium Maledictum explicitly separates ordinary Wounds, Critical Wounds, Injuries, Dying and Medical Treatment.

This is directly relevant to TRHGD's Character Life layer.

## TRHGD adaptation

Do not reproduce detailed Warhammer critical tables.

Use the structural distinction:

    ordinary damage
        ↓
    combat resource / HP

    serious defeat threshold
        ↓
    critical consequence

    critical consequence
        ↓
    temporary injury
    lasting scar
    body-slot injury
    death risk
    graft/prosthetic opportunity
    personal event

This works well with the proposed acquired-state architecture.

Possible injury record:

    injury:
        source_event
        body_slot
        severity
        temporary_effects
        lasting_effects
        treatment
        recovery_clock
        scar
        adaptation_options[]

---

# 10. Fate-like resources: surviving without trivializing danger

Warhammer RPGs frequently use limited fate/luck-style resources alongside lethal combat.

The important lesson is not the exact Fate Point rules.

It is that a game can support:

- genuinely dangerous combat;
- meaningful permanent consequences;
- attachment to long-lived characters;

without making a single unlucky roll routinely erase a character.

## TRHGD question for later

Consider whether the campaign needs a **very scarce survival resource** or explicit mortal-consequence choice.

Possible uses:

- avoid immediate death;
- convert death into a major injury;
- allow an ally to save the character at a cost;
- accept a curse/graft/transformation instead;
- force retreat with a serious consequence.

This should be rare enough that danger remains real.

It may be preferable to conventional save-scumming.

---

# 11. Dark Heresy: immediate shock → lasting trauma → corruption

Dark Heresy's designer material is particularly valuable.

Its Fear / Insanity / Corruption structure deliberately distinguishes:

1. immediate shock;
2. medium-term trauma;
3. rarer long-term disorders or corruption.

This is much better than a single "sanity meter".

## TRHGD adaptation

A horror event could produce different layers:

    EVENT
    encounter impossible/terrifying phenomenon

    IMMEDIATE
    panic / loss of action / retreat / temporary stress

    AFTERMATH
    nightmare / fatigue / short-lived fear response

    LONG-TERM, IF SEVERE
    persistent fear
    compulsion
    trauma aspect
    corruption exposure
    relationship consequence

The key is **rarity gradient**:

- immediate stress can be common;
- significant trauma should be less common;
- permanent psychological changes should be uncommon and story-worthy.

Avoid stigmatizing real-world mental illness or reducing characters to joke disorders.

Use fictional trauma responses that are contextual, legible and respectful.

---

# 12. Corruption should be separate from trauma

Dark Heresy makes a useful distinction between psychological trauma and supernatural corruption.

TRHGD should preserve this.

    TRAUMA
    what the mind has endured

    CORRUPTION
    what supernatural exposure has changed

A character can have one without the other.

For example:

- seeing a massacre may create trauma but no magical corruption;
- touching a cursed relic may create corruption without terror;
- encountering an eldritch entity may cause both.

This is cleaner than one "darkness" number.

---

# 13. Dark Pacts: voluntary damnation as player choice

Dark Heresy also explicitly supports characters entering dangerous supernatural bargains for deeply personal goals.

This is very strong inspiration for TRHGD.

A pact should begin with:

    I need something badly enough to accept a cost.

Possible motives:

- save another party member;
- cure an otherwise fatal wound;
- gain forbidden knowledge;
- defeat a specific enemy;
- protect the hometown;
- undo a personal tragedy.

Possible costs:

- corruption;
- obligation;
- taboo;
- future service;
- visible mark;
- relationship damage;
- escalating claim by the patron.

This gives pacts narrative weight rather than making them another feat tree.

---

# 14. Black Crusade: corruption as a race between power and destruction

Black Crusade goes further: corruption and supernatural gifts are part of an explicit risk/reward trajectory, with characters pursuing power while risking catastrophic transformation.

TRHGD should not copy its endpoint or morality assumptions, but the **race structure** is valuable:

    POWER GAIN
            ↘
             rising exposure / mutation / obligation
            ↗
    PLAYER DECISION

A corruption path can therefore have:

- benefits at thresholds;
- escalating risks;
- rare control/stabilization opportunities;
- an irreversible threshold;
- multiple possible endpoints.

This is much more interesting than:
"At 10 corruption you get -1."

---

# 15. Mutation does not automatically mean moral failure

Warhammer Fantasy material explicitly notes an important fiction distinction: someone who develops a mutation is not automatically a willing worshipper of Chaos. The social response may be brutal even when the character did not choose the change.

That distinction is useful for TRHGD.

A mutated character may face:

- fear;
- prejudice;
- concealment problems;
- specialist interest;
- faction suspicion;
- cult recruitment;
- rare communities that accept them.

But **the character's morality is not determined by their body**.

This fits the GAME-88 recommendation that mutation and corruption be acquired states rather than "evil points".

---

# 16. Mutation as social consequence

D&D-inspired mutation research mostly emphasizes mechanical body changes.

Warhammer adds an important second layer:

> What does society do when people can see the change?

TRHGD acquired states should therefore support:

    visibility:
        hidden
        concealable
        obvious
        unmistakable

    social_tags:
        feared
        forbidden
        sacred
        prestigious
        suspicious

The reaction should depend on **culture, faction, religion and locality**, not one global rule.

A fey mark could be prestigious in one region and terrifying in another.

---

# 17. Diseases should be campaign events, not just stat penalties

WFRP repeatedly treats disease as part of grim campaign life, and its GM material groups disease with corruption, mutation, critical wounds and long-term development.

TRHGD can use disease to connect:

- settlement conditions;
- travel;
- healers;
- facilities;
- downtime;
- regional events;
- party relationships.

A disease can create:

    symptoms
    incubation
    treatment decisions
    lost expedition time
    risk to companions
    local outbreak
    reputation for helping / abandoning settlement

This is more interesting than a poison-like temporary stat debuff.

---

# 18. Magic miscasts and dangerous power

WFRP's magic tradition consistently treats powerful magic as something that can go wrong, with miscasts and corruption part of the larger magical danger model.

TRHGD already plans telegraphed/delayed powerful magic from GAME-86.

Warhammer suggests another optional axis:

    safe casting
        lower effect / normal resource cost

    overchannel / desperate casting
        stronger or faster effect
        risk of backlash / corruption / uncontrolled consequence

Do **not** add this automatically.

It is worth testing later because it could give casters a dramatic emergency option without simply increasing everyday spell power.

---

# 19. Imperium Maledictum: Patron, Boons, Liabilities and Influence

Imperium Maledictum's party Patron architecture is extremely relevant to future TRHGD guild/faction play.

The group has a powerful Patron whose:

- faction;
- duty;
- motivation;
- demeanor;
- boons;
- liabilities;
- influence;

shape the campaign.

## TRHGD adaptation

TRHGD should not necessarily begin with a permanent patron.

But future campaigns could generate **sponsors / patrons / guild backers** such as:

- local noble;
- merchant consortium;
- temple;
- town council;
- retired adventurer;
- military officer;
- arcane society.

A patron can supply:

- access;
- equipment;
- information;
- legal protection;
- transport;
- specialists.

But every patron should also create pressure:

- rival faction;
- debt;
- political obligation;
- unpopular agenda;
- secrecy;
- demanded service.

The excellent structural lesson is:

    BOON
    must create
    LIABILITY / obligation / exposure

not necessarily one-for-one, but as a design philosophy.

---

# 20. Influence should be a network

Imperium Maledictum uses faction relationships and Influence to determine access and resources.

That strongly supports GAME-87's proposed scoped reputation.

TRHGD should eventually model reputation/influence as a graph:

    character / party / guild
        ↕
    town
    region
    faction
    religion
    guild
    noble house
    criminal group

Actions can improve one relationship while worsening another.

Avoid a universal Reputation = 72.

---

# 21. Investigation should create tactical advantage

Imperium Maledictum explicitly rewards successful investigation with mechanical advantage in later confrontations.

This is one of the most useful ideas for TRHGD's expedition loop.

It directly addresses the earlier GAME-84 problem:
**why are we investigating and how did individual party members help?**

A pre-combat expedition can produce structured advantages such as:

- reveal enemy type;
- reveal resistance/weakness;
- discover alternate entrance;
- reduce enemy reinforcement;
- improve starting deployment;
- identify a hazard;
- unlock a dialogue option;
- identify true target among decoys;
- reveal an illusion;
- gain surprise/preparation;
- place an environmental interaction on the battle map.

Then noncombat characters contribute meaningfully **before initiative is rolled**.

Example:

    Wayfinder:
    tracks raiders
    → reveals hidden approach
    → party gains alternative deployment zone

    Former mason:
    examines ruined tower
    → identifies unstable wall
    → battlefield contains collapsible breach

    Lantern Adept:
    interprets magical residue
    → identifies illusionist
    → one false target begins partially suspected

This is exactly the kind of bridge TRHGD needs between world exploration and GAME-33 combat.

---

# 22. Augmetics as a parallel to fantasy grafts/prosthetics

Imperium Maledictum includes augmetics, and its Adeptus Mechanicus material expands bodily replacement and technological corruption.

The relevant TRHGD lesson is the same as GAME-88's graft research:

**body replacement can be progression, treatment, identity and consequence at the same time.**

A fantasy analogue can include:

- crafted prosthetic;
- enchanted prosthetic;
- monster-derived graft;
- elemental limb;
- living symbiont;
- cursed replacement;
- divine relic prosthesis.

Each should occupy a body slot and have compatibility rules.

---

# 23. Hirelings with quirks and work ethics

WFRP's Up in Arms includes systems for hirelings with individual quirks and work ethics.

This is particularly relevant to future guild/recruitment work.

Generated NPC adventurers should not be only:

    Fighter level 3
    Salary 12

They can have:

- reliability;
- ambition;
- fear;
- loyalty;
- vice;
- work ethic;
- social ties;
- personal boundaries.

These should create manageable behavioral differences, not uncontrollable random AI.

# 24. The strongest combined D&D + Warhammer synthesis

The two research families complement each other unusually well.

## D&D 3.x contributes

- classes;
- multiclassing;
- feats;
- prestige;
- prerequisites;
- spells;
- grafts;
- templates;
- bloodlines;
- systematic build breadth.

## Warhammer RPG contributes

- careers as lived occupations;
- ambition;
- social standing;
- downtime;
- obligations;
- critical injuries;
- disease;
- corruption;
- mutation;
- fear/trauma;
- pacts;
- patrons;
- faction influence;
- investigation-to-confrontation payoff.

## Wildermyth contributes

- character-targeted events;
- relationships;
- hooks;
- lasting life history;
- transformations;
- emotional continuity.

The combined TRHGD identity becomes:

    D&D-LIKE BUILD
            +
    WARHAMMER-LIKE CONSEQUENCE
            +
    WILDERMYTH-LIKE CHARACTER HISTORY
            +
    TRHGD GENERATED WORLD
            +
    GAME-86/33 TACTICAL COMBAT

This is coherent as long as all three layers remain separated in data and joined through generic tags/effects/requirements.

# 26. Recommended systems to borrow conceptually first

Ranked by value to TRHGD:

1. **Investigation → confrontation advantage**
2. **Between-adventure Endeavours / scarce downtime**
3. **Career/occupation as fictional access separate from class**
4. **Critical injury → recovery/scar/adaptation**
5. **Fear → short-term stress → rare lasting trauma**
6. **Corruption separate from trauma**
7. **Mutation as body + social consequence**
8. **Patron boons/liabilities**
9. **Faction-specific Influence**
10. **Ambitions as event/quest weighting**
11. **Dangerous pacts**
12. **Rare magic overchannel/backlash**

The first six could significantly strengthen the game without requiring a giant new content library.

---

# 27. What to avoid

Do not copy Warhammer's setting assumptions or grimness mechanically.

Specifically avoid:

- mutation automatically meaning moral evil;
- constant random maiming;
- punitive attrition that makes characters disposable;
- forced corruption progression that overrides deliberate build choices;
- one-dimensional insanity caricatures;
- permanent penalties without adaptation opportunities;
- social systems so oppressive that the player cannot pursue the core adventure loop;
- dozens of currencies/meters added merely because source games have them;
- transplanting d100 mechanics into GAME-32.

TRHGD should borrow **structure and consequence**, not Warhammer's exact numerical systems.

---

# 28. Questions for GAME-87 Astra pass

When GAME-87 is resumed, Astra should explicitly compare this Warhammer research with the Wildermyth hypothesis.

Questions:

1. Should occupations/careers exist separately from GAME-32 classes?
2. How many downtime actions are enough to create meaningful choice without chores?
3. Can investigation advantages solve GAME-84's unclear-reward problem?
4. Should serious combat defeat use a Fate-like resource, a mortal-choice event, or both?
5. How should short-term stress and lasting trauma be separated?
6. Should corruption have bodily and psychic dimensions?
7. How should visible mutations interact with regional culture/reputation?
8. Can pacts be represented using the same relationship/aspect architecture as mentors and symbionts?
9. Should patrons become part of the guild system, campaign-start options, or only emergent contacts?
10. Which world facilities are required to make treatment/training/downtime meaningful?
11. How should critical injuries feed graft/prosthetic/transformational opportunities?
12. Which of these systems are high emotional value but low implementation cost?

# 30. Final recommendation

Warhammer RPG research should be **included in GAME-87**, but it does not justify reopening GAME-32 or GAME-86.

Its highest-value contribution is a second axis of progression:

> **Your class tells us what you deliberately learned.  
> Your history tells us what the world did to you.  
> Your career, status and obligations tell us where you now fit in that world.**

That is a strong foundation for the long-term TRHGD character experience.
